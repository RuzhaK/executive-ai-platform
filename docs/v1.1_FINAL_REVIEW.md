## Executive Summary

After reviewing a large sample of executive job alerts through the EMEA v1.1 pipeline — including 24 rows with explicit manual comments in column AL — the team concluded that **the workflow behaves as designed for a rule-based + AI-assisted CRM intake system**. The dominant source of “missed opportunities” is **employer-side country, authorization, language, and residency restrictions**, not fundamental workflow failure.

EMEA v1.1 delivers:

- Structured intake from LinkedIn job-alert emails  
- Deterministic pre-AI gates (language-in-title, preview threshold, location policy, enrichment, closed posting, travel, domain, language, country eligibility, Pre-PF eligibility on DEV baseline)  
- Tiered AI evaluation (Preview → Initial → Professional Fit → Verified)  
- Consistent CRM output with reject/skip/accept paths converging before Google Sheets append  

Most column AL comments are **calibration disagreements** (how high to score a role, whether Preview should open a posting) or **market limitations** (UK-only remote, Switzerland residency, mandatory local language). Two **confirmed implementation defects** (BUG-001, BUG-002) were identified during this review and **resolved in release commit `862e423`** before tag `v1.1-stable`.

**Executive Job CRM EMEA v1.1 is functionally complete and stable.**

**Version 1.1 is frozen. Future development will continue under v2 (Executive Opportunity Intelligence).**

## Why the project is frozen

After validating **several hundred executive jobs** through the EMEA v1.1 pipeline — including structured manual review of 24 annotated CRM rows — we concluded that the **primary limitation is no longer workflow quality** but **market constraints** that no amount of in-workflow tuning can remove:

- **Country-specific hiring** — roles advertised as “UK Remote”, “Germany Remote”, or country-list eligibility that excludes Bulgaria  
- **Work authorization** — explicit right-to-work, EU/UK nationality, or sponsorship-unavailable requirements  
- **Mandatory local language** — working-language mandates beyond English/Bulgarian  
- **Mandatory industry experience** — regulated or specialised domains (clinical, iGaming, deep cyber/GSI, enterprise architecture) where the candidate profile does not match  

The workflow correctly surfaces many of these as rejects or low scores. Remaining disagreements in column AL are predominantly **calibration** (how harshly to score a good role) rather than **missed detection**.

Further calibration of v1.1 — preview thresholds, Verified score bands, prompt wording — is expected to provide **diminishing returns** relative to effort. Each iteration improves marginal cases but does not address the structural filter: **most employers do not hire executives remotely into Bulgaria from a pan-European posting**.

Future effort should focus on **discovering companies that genuinely hire remotely across Europe** (or into Bulgaria/EU) before spending AI and review time on individual job postings. That is the mandate of **Executive Opportunity Intelligence (v2)**, not another v1.1 gate or prompt revision.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Separate role quality from employability.** Manual reviewers conflate “good role” with “can I apply from Bulgaria?” v1.1 architecture (Professional Fit → eligibility gates → Verified) is directionally correct; remaining noise is mostly employability.

2. **Preview gate is the cost lever.** Most calibration pain is Preview false negatives (Hyland, ENSEK) vs enrichment cost. Threshold changes are business decisions, not bugs.

3. **Guest HTML limits deterministic gates.** Closed posting detection fails when LinkedIn guest HTML omits markers (Mokrogoria). Document as ceiling; reproduction required before treating as defect.

4. **AI variability is bounded, not zero.** CleverMatch REJECT→ACCEPT without code change shows Verified path needs repeatability monitoring, not infinite prompt iteration.

5. **Sheet export bugs distort calibration.** XmlElement and invalid JSON rows look like scoring failures; engineering hygiene matters for manual review loops.

6. **24 annotated rows are sufficient for freeze.** 1,049 unannotated rows did not surface novel defect classes beyond calibration and market limits.

7. **Freeze is a product decision.** v1.1 workflow quality is adequate for production CRM intake; intelligence layer is the next multiplier.

### Freeze boundaries

- **In scope for v1.1 tag:** `Executive-Job-CRM-v1.1-STABLE.json` — frozen production-ready export; future EMEA work moves to Executive Opportunity Intelligence (v2).
- **Out of scope:** Strict Location multi-source JSON, preview/score calibration, new gates (C5, TZ), sheet schema expansion, policy redesign.
- **No further workflow redesign** under v1.1.

### Sign-off statement

> EMEA v1.1 meets the intended purpose: **reliable executive job CRM intake with deterministic rejection of clear blockers and AI-assisted triage**. Remaining column AL comments are predominantly calibration preferences or employer restrictions. Active workflow improvement stops at v1.1; strategic filtering moves to Executive Opportunity Intelligence (v2).

## Next Project

### Executive Opportunity Intelligence (v2)

Successor programme to Executive Job CRM v1.1. Shifts from **job-centric reactive intake** to **company-centric opportunity discovery**.

**Initial scope:**

- **Remote-first company database** — employers with verified pan-Europe or Bulgaria-eligible remote hiring patterns  
- **Company-centric monitoring** — watch lists, hiring velocity, leadership churn  
- **Career page monitoring** — executive role signals at source  
- **Executive hiring signals** — new C-suite / VP ops postings aggregated by company  
- **Career Strategy classification** — target vs bridge vs stretch roles aligned to long-term executive path  

v1.1 remains the **stable intake engine** for LinkedIn job-alert processing. v2 adds the intelligence layer that v1.1 validation showed is the real bottleneck.
